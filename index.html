About

I am an AI engineer and researcher with a background in statistics and programming. I recently completed an MSc in Mathematical Sciences, specialising in AI for Science, at the African Institute for Mathematical Sciences (AIMS) South Africa in partnership with Stellenbosch University, where I studied as a Google DeepMind Scholar and graduated with distinction. My MSc research developed an unsupervised, peer-relative representation learning approach for identifying energy-inefficient mobile network sites.

That experience shaped how I think about research: I like methods that are principled, but I also care whether they hold up when deployed.

I am now back at Onfon Mobile as an AI Research Engineer. Alongside that work I am exploring several areas of AI while building deeper expertise in the ones that interest me most, particularly representation learning, trustworthy machine learning and language technologies for African languages.

MSc research project

A Peer-Relative Representation Learning Framework for Energy Inefficiency Identification in Mobile Network Sites

AIMS South Africa & Stellenbosch University, 2026 · with Jaco du Toit, Adham Stoltz and Johan du Preez

Energy is a major operational cost for mobile network operators, but site-level inefficiencies are hard to spot: there are no ground-truth labels, and historical data already contains the inefficiencies one would like to find. The project treats this as an unsupervised problem and starts from a simple premise: comparable sites should consume comparable amounts of energy.

We formulate an energy-aware Minimum Distortion Embedding (MDE) with an energy-based repulsion term, so that sites consuming unusually more than their structural peers are displaced in the embedding space while overall similarity structure is preserved. The resulting peer-relative anomaly scores can be used to prioritise field investigations. On synthetic networks with varying levels of anomaly contamination, the approach compared favourably against standard anomaly-detection baselines such as Isolation Forest, Local Outlier Factor and autoencoders.

  2. Confidently Wrong: Measuring and Mitigating Calibration Risks in LLMs for African Languages

    Similoluwa Okunowo, Eliud Koto, Dagmawi Misker

    Preprint, 2026 · Global South AI Safety Hackathon (Apart Research & Schmidt Sciences), Africa regional winner · Presented as a workshop talk and poster at Deep Learning Indaba 2026

    Evaluates calibration and overconfidence in four open-weight LLMs across African-language benchmarks. Finds that models become systematically more overconfident, and less well calibrated, when moving from English to African languages, and evaluates per-language temperature scaling as a cheap, deployment-time mitigation that needs no retraining.
